# Add about 45 programs, mostly India-native and annual

The directory has room for **about 45 new programs**. Most are India-native and most run one cycle a year. As of 3 Nov 2026, few of them are open. Five are open now or close soon and should be listed first: the **IndiaAI Fellowship** (deadline 30 Nov 2026), the **NIF Book Fellowship** (₹18 lakh; deadline somewhere between 15 Nov and December 2026), **ANRF PM-ECRG** (up to ₹60 lakh; window probably 2 Nov–2 Dec 2026), **Obama Foundation Leaders: Asia-Pacific** (applications open 9 Nov) and **Teach For India**, whose later rounds may still be open. Most other programs, including IFA, the Ministry of Culture fellowships, LAMP, SBI YFI, the state CM fellowships, iDEX and Echoing Green, have closed their 2026 cycles. They should be listed as "closed – annual" with the month they usually open. Only a few India-native programs fund a person in any field. The Inlaks Fellowship for Social Engagement is the best fit, and Rise and Thiel are the only verified global options with Indian winners. The neurotech tag is the weakest: I found no India-dedicated neurotech grant. One caveat applies to everything below. The researchers mostly read search snippets and aggregator sites, not the official pages, so **every status and amount needs a check against the official URL before it is published**. Confidence is marked High (official page or several sources agree), Medium (one good source or aggregators agree) or Low (sources conflict, or only an aggregator).

## Only a few programs fund a person in any field

Most "any field" money in India pays for a degree rather than funding a person, so this category stays small. The **Inlaks Shivdasani Fellowship for Social Engagement** is the best India-native fit. It supports a two-year, self-designed learning project and its last cycle closed 31 Dec 2025 ([fundsforngos](https://www2.fundsforngos.org/health/applications-open-for-the-inlaks-fellowship-for-social-engagement-india/)). Rise (Schmidt Futures and the Rhodes Trust) is for 15–17-year-olds and offers a summit, a need-based scholarship and project funding. Its **2022 global cohort included 16 Indian students** ([The Hitavada](https://thehitavada.com/Encyc/2022/9/28/Rudraksha-selected-for-Int-l-RISE-Fellowship-Programme.html)), but its current cycle status is unknown. The Thiel Fellowship pays **$250k over two years with no equity**, starting with the 2026 class, to applicants aged 22 or under. Applications are rolling, and Ritesh Agarwal of OYO is a past fellow ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/thiel-foundation-2026-thiel-fellowship-class-awarded-250000-grants-to-young-entrepreneurs/); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/thiel-20-under-20-two-indians-make-it-to-elite-group/)). In practice Thiel funds builders, so it can sit under Open or Entrepreneurship.

## Entrepreneurship: government grants take no equity, talent investors do

India's strongest non-dilutive startup money comes from government schemes, and most of it reaches founders through incubators. Each entry should say "apply via a host incubator" and link the official portal. The **Startup India Seed Fund Scheme** (₹945 cr, any sector, startups no more than 2 years old) is still running, but whether applications are open is disputed ([Guidelines](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/dam/invest-india/Templates/public/Guidelines%20for%20Startup%20India%20Seed%20Fund%20Scheme.pdf); [Sarkariyojana](https://sarkariyojana.com/startup-india-seed-fund-scheme/)). **iDEX** runs defence challenges worth up to ₹25 cr (DISC 14), but every 2026 deadline has passed ([iDEX](https://idex.gov.in/disc-category/48)). The talent investors (Entrepreneur First, Antler and South Park Commons) **all take equity**. Antler's reported terms are ₹4 cr for about 11% ([startupgrantsindia](https://www.startupgrantsindia.com/providers/antler-india)), and SPC invests $1M ([SPC FAQ](https://www.southparkcommons.com/faq)). The directory needs an equity flag so these can be told apart from grants.

## Research money depends on a host institution, and neurotech has no India-specific grant

Almost every India-native research grant needs a host institution. That rules out independent researchers for most of the money. **ANRF PM-ECRG** (up to ₹60 lakh, age limit 42) and the **N-PDF** (~₹80,000/month, 2026 call closed in February) both require one ([ANRF](https://anrfonline.in/ANRF/ecrg_anrf); [India S&T portal](https://www.indiascienceandtechnology.gov.in/announcementsopportunity/national-post-doctoral-fellowship-n-pdf-2026)). So does the **India Alliance Early Career Fellowship**, which pays up to ₹2.25 crore over 5 years with no age or nationality limit ([IndiaBioscience](https://indiabioscience.org/orgs/the-wellcome-trust-dbt-india-alliance/jobs/early-career-fellowship---basic)). The only routes open to students or people without an institution are the IndiaAI Fellowship, BIRAC BIG (a bachelor's degree plus an incubator) and DBT iBEC for undergraduate iGEM teams. Several global corporate PhD fellowships have India tracks with named Indian winners: Google (an IIT Hyderabad scholar in 2025, [IITH](https://iith.ac.in/main-highlights/2025/10/27/siddharth-shrivastava-google-phd-fellowship/)), Qualcomm (IISc in 2024, [IISc CDS](https://cds.iisc.ac.in/ph-d-student-abhipsa-basu-receives-the-2024-qualcomm-innovation-fellowship-india/)) and NVIDIA (Ahmedabad University faculty, [Ahmedabad Univ](https://ahduni.edu.in/academics/schools-centres/school-of-engineering-and-applied-science/research/funded-projects/ratnik-gandhi-and-sanjay-chaudhary-receive-research-grants-from-nvidia-corporation/)). The global philanthropic funders mostly fail the Indian-recipient test. **No India-dedicated neurotech grant exists** in the notes. Neurotech applicants can only use India Alliance (biomedical neuroscience) and ANRF. The **open-source tag also has no new candidates**: NLnet needs a European dimension and has no Indian grantees found, and FOSS United and FLOSS/fund are already in the directory.

## Policy and civic: paid India-native fellowships, with the state CM programmes paying most

This category has the most well-paid India-native options. PRS's **LAMP** places people aged 25 or under with MPs for ₹23,000 a month. Two well-known programs fit "funded" poorly. The Young India Fellowship charges about ₹8 lakh in fees ([Ashoka](https://www.ashoka.edu.in/yif-scholarships-and-fee/)), and Acumen India pays no stipend.

## Degree scholarships belong in a separate category

Several large programs fund a degree rather than a project or a person. Listing them under Open would blur what the directory offers. They are the **Inlaks scholarships** (up to $120k; excludes CS, engineering, MBA and medicine; [Inlaks](https://www.inlaksfoundation.org/scholarships/)), the **JN Tata Endowment** (interest-free loan up to ₹20 lakh; likely opens Jan 2027; [indiascholarships](https://www.indiascholarships.in/scholarships/jn-tata-endowment-for-higher-education)) and **K.C. Mahindra** (2026 cycle closed 30 Apr; [KCMET](https://kcmet.org/what-we-do-Scholarship-Grants.aspx)). Others are **Reliance Foundation** (5,000 undergraduate awards up to ₹2 lakh; closed 5 Oct; [Reliance Foundation](https://www.reliancefoundation.org/media/media-release/Reliance_Foundation_Scholarships_2026-27)), **Fulbright-Nehru Master's** (next round ~Feb–May 2027) and the Young India Fellowship. I recommend a separate "Scholarships / degree funding" category for them.

## Conclusion

The directory would be most useful if it showed when each program opens, not just whether it is open today. Most Indian programs run one fixed cycle a year, so on most dates the directory will show nearly everything as closed. Adding a "usually opens" month to every entry and showing upcoming deadlines first would fix this. Two structural fields matter just as much: an **equity flag**, so that EF, Antler and SPC are not mistaken for grants, and a **host-institution or incubator requirement**, because ANRF, India Alliance, SISFS and NIDHI all exclude independent applicants. The biggest gaps in what India offers are a no-strings neurotech grant and an open-source fund that Indians can actually win. Those are real gaps, and the directory can say so instead of filling them with global programs that have no Indian recipients.
